Synthetic customers overstated demand, and none beat a gradient-boosted model.

Do synthetic users predict what real customers do next, or only sound plausible? Two held-out tests on real retail transactions, with the protocol and analysis code committed before any model output existed (commit 4ac31dc).

3,968test rows
9,792scored outcomes
9conditions per outcome
gpt-5.4-minimain model

Findings

  1. No LLM condition beat gradient boosting. Structured LLM minus GBDT Brier: purchase +0.0176 [+0.0142, +0.0212], coupons +0.0220 [+0.0167, +0.0273]. GBDT 0.1571 vs 0.1747 and 0.0927 vs 0.1148.
  2. Adding the LLM to gradient boosting did not help. Hybrid minus GBDT: purchase +0.0036 [+0.0023, +0.0049], coupons +0.0029 [+0.0003, +0.0053]; practically small and in the wrong direction.
  3. Personas predicted worse than the record they were written from. Persona minus structured (97.5% CI): purchase +0.0550 [+0.0472, +0.0628], coupons +0.0306 [+0.0245, +0.0364].
  4. The persona penalty is mostly over-optimism. Persona-based customers predicted a 52% purchase rate (actual 33%) and 32% coupon redemption (actual 14%). After validation calibration the gap shrinks to +0.0049 [+0.0015, +0.0081] and +0.0031 [-0.0010, +0.0070].
  5. Simulated customers did not rank campaigns reliably. Rank correlation with observed redemption across nine campaigns: GBDT 0.55, structured LLM 0.13, persona -0.10. Descriptive; nine points.
  6. A frontier model fixed much of the persona gap, not the baseline gap. On a subsample, gpt-5.5-2026-04-23 persona Brier was 0.1806 vs 0.2499 for the main model (purchase), but GBDT stayed ahead (0.1681; coupons 0.1169 vs 0.1459 structured).

Related work and what is new here

Evaluating LLM "synthetic users" against real people is an active area. OPeRA benchmarks next-action prediction in real web shopping sessions; When Synthetic Users Fail finds that no LLM beats the strongest non-LLM baseline at the individual level on survey data. This study is consistent with that finding and extends it to longitudinal purchase records and responses to real marketing campaigns, with a same-source persona ablation, validation-only calibration and stacking, campaign-level fidelity, and a protocol committed before any model output existed.

Deviations from the protocol

  • The provider limits queued tokens per model. Batches were therefore split into sequential parts; two submissions were rejected by that limit before any request ran and were resubmitted unchanged. No processed request was retried.
  • Failed or unparsable requests: 0. Rows dropped: study A 0, study B 0.
  • Headline wording is generated from the results after they existed; the contrasts, intervals and all numbers follow the frozen code.

How to read this

Brier score is mean squared error of a probability; lower is better. H1 compares a persona written from the shopping record with the record itself. H2 asks whether adding the LLM to a strong tabular model helps. Intervals resample households, so repeated observations of one household are not treated as independent.

One retailer (dunnhumby Complete Journey, public, 2-year anonymised panel); models may have seen this public dataset during training; one draw per request, so run-to-run stability is not measured; prompts are one realistic design, not an optimised persona pipeline. Independent research.
